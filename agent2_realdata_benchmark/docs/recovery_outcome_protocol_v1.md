# Independent Recovery Outcome Protocol — Version 1

Status: Draft research protocol.
Supervisor approval: Not established.
Human study approval: Not established.
Implementation status: Not implemented.

## 1. Research question

Does selecting a pedagogical intervention improve
subsequent independently assessed performance
compared with an appropriate control policy?

This document specifies a candidate outcome.
It does not establish an intervention effect.

## 2. Unit of assessment

A learner has an eligible difficulty event
associated with a prespecified target concept.

Eligibility rules must be fixed before evaluation.

Repeated events from the same learner require
appropriate handling in the study design.

## 3. Primary outcome

Y = 1 if the learner answers the independent
target-concept probe correctly on the first attempt.

Y = 0 if the learner answers it incorrectly
on the first attempt.

The probe is administered without hints.

This is probe success, not proof of durable mastery.

## 4. Probe independence

The probe must be a different item from the
question that triggered the difficulty event.

It must assess the prespecified target concept.

Difficulty and probe-form allocation must be
controlled across comparison groups.

The probe must not be used to generate the
intervention or diagnose the triggering event.

Its answer must not enter the learner-state
model before the primary outcome is recorded.

## 5. Measurement timing

Use a prespecified assessment slot after one
standardized intervention opportunity.

Comparison groups must receive an equivalent
assessment schedule.

The precise time limit and item allocation
must be fixed before collecting outcome data.

They are not selected in this draft.

## 6. Missing outcomes

A missing probe response is not an observed
correct or incorrect answer.

Record separately:

- Probe presented but unanswered.
- Learner left before the scheduled probe.
- Technical failure.
- Other documented missingness.

The primary missing-data strategy and
sensitivity analyses must be preregistered.

Do not silently discard missing outcomes.

## 7. Comparison and effect estimation

A causal intervention effect cannot be
identified from probe success alone.

A suitable randomized comparison can estimate
the effect of an assigned intervention policy,
subject to the specified protocol and assumptions.

Use a prespecified analysis population.

Account for repeated observations per learner.

No effect size is currently established.

## 8. Meaning of recovery

Correctness on one independent probe provides
limited evidence of successful performance.

It does not establish:

- Durable mastery.
- Long-term retention.
- Causal benefit without a comparison.
- Satisfaction of a latent-state drift condition.

A stronger individual recovery definition requires
a prespecified baseline and assessment criterion.

## 9. Existing evidence

Phase 6 observational follow-ups do not provide
this independent randomized recovery outcome.

The recovery controller is proposal-based.

Execution remains simulation-only.

The 45 execution tests do not establish
educational effectiveness.

## 10. Decisions awaiting approval

- Eligible learner population.
- Target concepts and probe bank.
- Probe difficulty and form allocation.
- Assessment timing.
- Comparison groups and randomization.
- Missing-data analysis.
- Sample-size calculation.
- Ethics and consent arrangements.

## 11. Evidence boundaries

No human recovery result is established.

No causal intervention effect is established.

No frozen experimental artifact is modified.

The held-out test gate remains closed.
