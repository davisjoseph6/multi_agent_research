# Step 108 — Order domination via Blackwell garbling: proof, boundary and novelty gate

**Date:** 2026-10-31. **Status:** Pencil-and-paper research note. **Not an IJCAI novelty claim, empirical finding, calibrated student model, or authorization to enroll humans.** No algorithm/source changes.

## 1. Why Step 107 is useful but insufficient

Step 107 gives an exact two-question binary example with a fully restorative teaching assessment A and nonteaching assessment B. With both questions mandatory and equal priors on **initial** mastery, the Bayes error is 7/40 when A precedes B and 101/800 when B precedes A. Under a separate cost-sensitive **adaptive stopping** objective, the optimum is 71/500. All these values are *model-specific*, and this is not itself a theorem about past forgetting.

The correct research question is whether ordering has **uniform decision-theoretic value**, and what assumptions make that value disappear. The natural method is Blackwell's existing information comparison theorem—not a purported new machine-learning result.

## 2. Ordering proposition (an application of established Blackwell theory)

Let H∈{M,U} be mastery **before** two assessments. Let A and B be distinct items, each used exactly once.

Assume:

1. **Pre-transition emissions.** For X∈{A,B} and current mastery m∈{M,U}, the answer Y_X is sampled from an arbitrary finite-alphabet response channel O_X(·|m), before any learning effect caused by X.
2. **B is passive.** Administering B does not change mastery.
3. **A only restores.** If mastery is M, A leaves it M. If mastery is U, after observing Y_A=a, A restores mastery with probability ρ(a)∈[0,1], independent of Y_B given H and Y_A. In particular, ρ may depend on the answer; there is no forgetting or other latent change between the two actions.
4. **Stable emissions.** The B observation channel O_B(·|m) does not depend on order, elapsed time, response history or intervention beyond the current mastery m. The A emission depends on current mastery and not on B's observed answer. Conditional sampling follows the stated mechanisms.
5. **Forced equal exposure.** Both orders administer *both* unique items. Their total cost is the same, and A has the same final-state transition law when placed first or second.

**Proposition (a standard Blackwell-garbling argument).** The experiment generated by order **B then A** is Blackwell at least as informative about **initial** mastery H as the experiment generated by order **A then B**. Consequently, for *any prior on H and any finite terminal decision/loss function depending only on H*, the optimal expected terminal loss following B→A is no larger than following A→B. Both full schedules induce the same distribution of final mastery conditional on H and Y_A.

**Proof with an explicit, H-independent garbling kernel.** In the B→A experiment, the actual observed pair is (b,a). First relabel it (a,b) to put the A response first. Then, using only the observed a and b, form a simulated B-after-A response b' as follows:

- With probability 1−ρ(a), set b'=b.
- With probability ρ(a), independently draw b' from O_B(·|M).

Call this stochastic operation K((a,b')|(b,a)). K depends on observed a and known model parameters only; it **does not depend on the unknown hypothesis H**.

If H=M, both real and simulated B responses have channel O_B(·|M), regardless of whether replacement occurred. Conditional on Y_A=a the result equals the true A→B law. If H=U, the actual B-before-A response has channel O_B(·|U), while the hypothetical B-after-A response must have channel

    (1−ρ(a)) O_B(·|U) + ρ(a) O_B(·|M),

and this is precisely what K produces, conditional on Y_A=a. The joint distribution of (a,b') therefore equals the A→B joint observation distribution for **each** H. This establishes a Blackwell garbling of B→A into A→B.

Any terminal classifier/decision rule for A→B can be simulated by a decision maker seeing B→A: run K and apply that rule. Thus optimal expected loss under B→A cannot be higher, for every prior and terminal loss. Both sequences execute A once, with the same conditional distribution for Y_A and ρ(Y_A), so they also have the same final mastery distribution. QED.

**Important scope:** "Blackwell at least as informative" does not imply a **strict** improvement for every loss and prior. If ρ=0, the two experiments are informationally equivalent up to order; if the first response determines H perfectly, an additional informative signal may have no value for zero–one loss. The proof is not a new theorem—it uses the classical Blackwell comparison of statistical experiments.

## 3. Cross-check with exact Step 107 numerical example

For the Step 107 numbers, O_A and O_B have mastered correctness 9/10 and unmastered correctness 1/4, with ρ≡1.

The garbling kernel discards the observed pre-learning B response and generates an independent B-after-A response from Bernoulli(9/10). Thus after A, B conveys no information about H, whereas before A it provides a second independent diagnostic signal.

- Fixed order A→B: equal-prior Bayes error 7/40 = 0.175.
- Fixed order B→A: equal-prior Bayes error 101/800 = 0.12625.
- Strict difference: 39/800 = 0.04875.
- Equal final mastery: both orders reach current M with probability one.
- Cost-sensitive adaptive stopping (cost 0.01 per used item) yields a different objective and optimum 71/500, with lower final mastery probability than using both questions.

The general Blackwell proposition only compares the **forced-two-action orderings**. It does **not** say that every adaptive POMDP policy should delay teaching. In particular, the adaptive problem may prefer early treatment when final mastery, opportunity costs, fading items, or forgetting enter its reward and feasibility constraints.

## 4. What would invalidate the argument?

The structural hypotheses are deliberately strong. A failure of any one does **not by itself** prove that the reverse order is better; it means the kernel constructed above is not justified and a new analysis is required.

| Changed real-world mechanism | Why the proof no longer follows automatically |
|---|---|
| B itself teaches or induces forgetting | The A answer after B is no longer sampled from the same initial-state A emission |
| A can harm a mastered learner, with state-dependent nonlinear updates | The simple "resample from mastered" garbling can fail |
| Slips/guesses or question difficulty change after instruction independent of mastery | The B-after-A response may not equal O_B(·|M) or O_B(·|U) |
| Adaptive question availability, prerequisites, deadlines or learner fatigue | Both complete orders may not be feasible or have equal costs/outcome timing |
| Item-specific response correlations or history-dependent teaching | The joint response law need not factor through the garbling kernel |
| Goal is diagnosis of an actual latent forgetting **path event**, not H | The event can occur between questions, so the comparison needs additional hidden-path state and proof |
| Learning/forgetting model parameters are unknown and action choice affects their identification | The known-model stochastic kernel may be unavailable or non-robust |

### One precise robustness obligation

If the policy does not know ρ or O_B(·|M), then the *proof* shows Blackwell dominance separately for each fixed known θ, with a θ-specific garbling K_θ. This **does not automatically furnish one θ-independent garbling kernel** valid over an uncertainty set Θ. For a minimax or model-agnostic policy comparison, one must construct a **single** feasible history transformation/decision rule, or account for model estimation. It would be an error to assert "uniform robust dominance" merely by applying the known-model theorem separately to every model.

But failing to find a common garbling is **not sufficient to prove a new robust-POMDP theorem or a reversal**. We would need a concrete model family, exact risk criterion, a lower bound and an executable policy with an appropriate independent comparison.

## 5. Main-track novelty decision

The mathematical characterization above is an instance of **Blackwell (1951/1953)** and a standard terminal-decision ordering comparison. It is not a novel IJCAI contribution.

Prior tutoring work already discusses quizzes for diagnosis, feedback for instruction and planning their sequences; see **Rafferty et al., *Faster Teaching via POMDP Planning*, Cognitive Science 2016**. The existence of a general information-order comparison should make us **more cautious**, not more confident, about a "new teaching/diagnosis order" claim.

A potentially meaningful **next** question (still *not* an established open problem) is:

> When noisy, nonreusable probes have response-dependent teaching and sometimes cause forgetting, and the teacher must decide whether a **historical forgetting event** occurred under a structured ambiguity set Θ, can one derive a computable, nonvacuous robust diagnosis–recovery bound or an efficient policy that materially improves on the best current controlled-sensing/robust-POMDP method?

The first requirement is to **find a counterexample** to any proposed simple ordering rule that respects all claimed model assumptions. If the proposed generalization is immediately implied by a Blackwell garbling, standard data-processing inequality, or ordinary Bellman recursion, do not submit it as novel.
